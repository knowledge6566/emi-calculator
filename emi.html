How EMI is calculated

An EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is a fixed amount you pay each month until the loan is fully repaid. Each payment covers the interest for that month first, and the rest reduces your principal.

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1)
P = loan amount, r = monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100), n = number of months

Because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, early EMIs are mostly interest and later EMIs are mostly principal.

Frequently asked questions

Does a longer tenure reduce my EMI?

Yes, a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI, but you pay more interest overall. Compare a few tenures with the sliders above to see the trade-off.

What happens if I prepay?

Prepaying reduces the outstanding principal, which lowers future interest. Ask your lender whether prepayment charges apply.

Is this calculator accurate for home loans?

It uses the standard reducing-balance formula used by most most banks. Your actual schedule may differ slightly because of fees, insurance, or floating-rate changes.
